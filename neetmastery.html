The Illusion

3 March 2024. 5:00 AM.

The exam was in two months. This was my second attempt at NEET, and I had already decided something: This would be my last.

"I was sitting at my desk staring at the wall."

The day before, I spent 16 hours studying electrostatics. Not the first time. Not even the second. I already had detailed notes, so I didn't write anything new—I just watched the video lectures carefully and tried to remember everything.

After the lectures, I wrote out the formulas. Again and again. Derivations, constants, equations. I wrote them until it felt burned into my brain. Then I solved PYQs alongside solution videos.

"By the end of the day, I felt completely satisfied. For once, it felt like I was doing things properly. Like maybe this time it would finally work."

The Crash

4 May 2024.

I was sitting in the exam hall, pen in hand. Outside, my mother was waiting in the heat. She had seen how hard I worked this time. She believed today would be different.

The paper started. I turned the page. And there it was: a question from electrostatics.

I read it. Then I read it again. It felt so familiar. I knew I had seen something just like this. I sat there trying to force my brain to remember the lectures, the notes, the formulas I had written dozens of times.

"But nothing came. No formula. No method. Just a strange, sickening feeling of familiarity. Like seeing someone’s face but not remembering their name."

With every question I couldn’t solve, something inside me started fading. Confidence. Hope. One blank bubble on the OMR sheet at a time.

And that’s when something terrifying hit me: I didn’t fail because I didn’t study hard. I failed because I didn’t know how to learn.

The Discovery

Refusing to let it go.

I was so frustrated, almost depressed. Those early mornings, late nights, dozens of books filled with notes, hours spent watching lectures—and worst of all, failing my parents' hopes.

I decided to really understand why I failed.

What did the toppers do differently? I put in the hours, so there had to be something else. I searched everywhere. I watched topper interviews, read books, articles, and cognitive research papers to understand exactly how the human brain retains information.

The answers were clear as the night sky. And they made me realize that everything we consider "right"—reading, re-reading, highlighting, and solving the exact same types of questions until we perfect them—is actually a complete waste of time.

It creates a false sense of mastery. It makes things look familiar, but it doesn't build long-term memory.

I wished someone had actually taught me the right way to study before I even picked up my biology book. Since nobody did, I decided to build it myself. Something short and sweet that you can finish in 2 hours, but will change how you prepare forever.
